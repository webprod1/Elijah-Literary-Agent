01 / PERSPECTIVE

Fiction & Literary Storytelling

Fiction creates its own terms of attention. Character, structure, setting, and voice need to work together so that an imagined world feels emotionally convincing. A considered reading asks what kind of experience the manuscript offers and how its opening prepares the reader for that experience.

02 / PERSPECTIVE

Narrative Nonfiction & Personal Narratives

Narrative nonfiction and memoir bring additional questions of structure, perspective, and responsibility. A compelling personal experience is a starting point; shaping it into a book involves deciding what the reader needs to know, which scenes carry the argument, and where reflection belongs.

03 / PERSPECTIVE

Author Development & Editorial Preparation

Development can involve a manuscript, a proposal, or the way an author introduces a project. Feedback is most useful when it identifies priorities and explains why a change might help. Preparation should leave a writer with a clearer sense of the work, not simply a longer list of tasks.

04 / PERSPECTIVE

Market Positioning & Professional Communication

Positioning connects a particular work with a plausible readership. It considers category, comparable titles, and the language used in a query or proposal. Professional communication should make the book easier to understand while remaining accurate about its stage and ambitions.

HOW THE WORK TAKES SHAPE

01

Literary Representation

Strategic guidance for authors seeking to position their work within a competitive publishing landscape.

A considered view of your manuscript, your intended readership, and the opportunities worth exploring. Representation begins with a conversation about fit, expectations, and the work itself.

02

Editorial Development

Helping promising manuscripts reach their fullest potential through constructive editorial direction.

Structure, pace, argument, and voice all shape a reader’s experience. Editorial development looks at these elements together, identifying changes that serve the book rather than imposing a formula.

03

Publishing Strategy

Navigating publishing opportunities with clarity, preparation, and a long-term perspective.

A useful strategy connects creative ambitions with practical choices. We consider the project’s stage, its category, and the materials needed to communicate its value clearly.

04

Author Advocacy

Building relationships founded on communication, creative integrity, and respect for the author’s vision.

Writers need space to create and clarity about decisions. Thoughtful advocacy means asking useful questions, explaining options, and keeping the author’s priorities at the center of the conversation.

BEYOND A SINGLE MANUSCRIPT

A career is more
than one chapter.

Long-term literary development includes the space between projects: what an author wants to explore next, which commitments are sustainable, and how professional choices fit a creative life. A plan should be clear enough to guide action and flexible enough to respond to what the work reveals.

Publishing and media opportunities depend on many independent decisions. Thoughtful guidance supports preparation and evaluation; it does not guarantee a book deal, distribution, coverage, or any particular outcome.

QUESTIONS, CONSIDERED

A little clarity.

What does literary representation involve?+

In general, representation may include editorial discussion, market positioning, submission planning, and professional advocacy within an agreed scope. The details of any relationship should be discussed and documented before work begins.

Is my manuscript ready for consideration?+

Readiness depends on the project and the recipient’s requirements. A completed fiction manuscript should usually have undergone substantive revision. Nonfiction may require a proposal and sample material. Ask about the appropriate next step before sending a full manuscript.

Which genres does Elijah accept?+

This website does not publish a confirmed genre wish list. Use the contact page to describe your project and ask whether it may be suitable for consideration. The educational topics on this page are not submission categories.

Does an inquiry establish representation?+

No. Sending an inquiry does not create a representation relationship. Any proposed collaboration requires a separate discussion and an agreed scope.

Where should I send my inquiry?+

Use the contact page to prepare an email draft, or write directly to elijah.cruz@independentliteraryagent.net. Include a brief introduction to the work and the kind of guidance you are seeking.
