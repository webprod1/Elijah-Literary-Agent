Preparing Your Manuscript for Professional Consideration

A professional submission makes the work easy to encounter. Thoughtful preparation gives the pages room to speak.

ELIJAH CRUZ BLOG · PUBLISHING PERSPECTIVES

Finish the right kind of revision

Before polishing every sentence, consider the larger structure. Does the opening establish a meaningful direction? Do scenes or chapters develop rather than repeat the same point? Is the ending earned by what precedes it? Correcting commas will not resolve a missing narrative turn or an unsupported argument.

Create a short revision plan that separates structural questions from line-level work. Address the changes with the greatest effect first, then review continuity and language. A trusted reader can help identify where the intended experience differs from the actual one.

Prepare materials with a clear purpose

A query introduces the project and explains why a reader might want to see more. A synopsis communicates the narrative arc, including the resolution when requested. A nonfiction proposal explains the book’s idea, audience, structure, and the author’s basis for writing it. These documents perform different jobs.

Use the recipient’s current instructions to determine what to send. Requirements differ, and a polished package can still be unsuitable if it omits requested material or includes attachments that are not accepted.

Make the final pass practical

Use readable formatting, consistent chapter headings, and a sensible file name. Check that contact information is correct and that the version you attach is the version you intend to share. Read your introductory email separately from the manuscript so small administrative errors receive proper attention.

Keep a private record of where and when you submit. Preparation cannot guarantee a particular response, but it can reduce avoidable confusion and help you approach each conversation with confidence.

General educational perspective. Requirements vary by recipient and project; consult current submission instructions before sending materials.
