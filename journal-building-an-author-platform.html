Building a Strong Author Platform

An author platform is most useful when it grows from a clear purpose and a genuine relationship with readers.

ELIJAH EDITORIAL JOURNAL · PUBLISHING PERSPECTIVES

Start with the reader, not the channel

A platform connects an author’s work with people who may value it. Before choosing a newsletter, a speaking format, or a social channel, identify the conversations your work contributes to. A precise sense of readership makes it easier to decide what to share and where to share it.

For some authors, a focused website and occasional essays may be appropriate. Others may already have a community through teaching, professional work, or local events. There is no single platform shape that suits every book.

Build a body of useful work

A clear biography, accurate project descriptions, and a small collection of thoughtful writing give readers a way to understand your interests. Consistency helps, but a demanding posting schedule can take time away from the manuscript it is supposed to support.

Choose a pace you can sustain. An occasional substantive contribution can do more to communicate a perspective than frequent posts written only to remain visible.

Measure the quality of the connection

Audience size is one part of a larger picture. Consider whether people read, respond, return, or share useful questions. Those signals can help an author understand what resonates without turning every creative decision into a pursuit of attention.

Keep claims about reach accurate, respect subscribers’ privacy, and make it easy to understand what a reader is signing up for. A platform is an ongoing relationship, not a substitute for the work.
